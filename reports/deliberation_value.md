# Meta-controller deliberation value — do teacher trees have low search value?

**Ref:** `R-MC-DELIB` · [Index](reference.md)

## The Question

Do the search trees generated by a highly trained teacher policy have near-zero deliberation value, and does this make optimal stopping a trivial, budget-determined countdown?

We hypothesize that because the Leela/lc0 policy prior is extremely strong, the move recommended at expansion 1 is very frequently the same move recommended at expansion 96. If search rarely changes the recommended move, the actual **value of computation (Gain)** is flat across almost all states. In this regime, additional search time confers no benefit, making the optimal stoppage almost always "stop immediately" (or at a fixed budget fraction).

---

## Procedural Proposal: Instability and Policy-Prior Ablation

We propose to isolate and test this hypothesis by evaluating the GNN-MC on subsets where the ex-ante policy is unstable, or by generating trees using a weaker policy head.

### Experiment A: PUCT-Instability Filtering
We will evaluate whether the GNN-MC outperforms the budget-only floor specifically on positions where search actively corrects a policy error.
* **Procedure:**
  1. Filter the cached tree dataset to include only **PUCT-unstable** positions where the ex-ante recommended move (at step 1) **differs** from the final search recommendation (at step 96).
  2. Evaluate the pre-trained budget-only floor and the full GNN-MC model on this subset.
  3. Compare their regrets. If the GNN-MC outperforms the floor specifically on these "correction-required" positions, it proves that the representation $z$ is valuable, but its utility is masked by the high proportion of "already-solved" positions in the full dataset.

### Experiment B: High-Temperature Tree Generation
We will test if the GNN-MC's stopping utility increases when search is forced to do more "work" to resolve the best move.
* **Procedure:**
  1. Generate a small test set of trees (e.g., 2,000 trees) using a higher policy temperature (e.g., `--temperature 1.5` or `1.8` in `cts.data.build_tree`) or an older, weaker Leela checkpoint.
  2. This weakens the policy prior, forcing PUCT to explore more widely and making the value of deeper deliberation higher.
  3. Regenerate the DP oracle labels for this high-temperature dataset.
  4. Train and evaluate the models on this new dataset.
  5. Check if the GNN-MC achieves a significant lift over the budget-only floor compared to the standard, low-temperature dataset.

---

## Expected Outcomes
* If **Theory 3 (Low Deliberation Value)** is correct, the GNN-MC will show a clear, measurable lift over the budget-only floor on the PUCT-unstable subset (Experiment A) and on the high-temperature dataset (Experiment B).
* This would confirm that representation-based stopping is highly valuable in unstable or uncertain positions, but its global average utility is diluted by the extreme strength of the teacher's ex-ante policy.
* It would suggest that the meta-controller should be deployed selectively, or trained on a curriculum that emphasizes tactically unstable positions.
